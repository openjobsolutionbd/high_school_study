Read the text and answer the questions 1 & 2

Helen Keller was a great humanitarian. She cared deeply for the sick, injured and disabled people. The disabled people have physical or mental problems that limit their activities or senses. Helen devoted her life to help them.

Helen was born in 1880 in Alabama, USA. Her parents were Captain Arthur Keller and Katherine Adams Keller. Her family was not rich. Their main source of income was cotton plantation.

Helen was born healthy, but in 1882 she became deaf, dumb and blind after a high fever. Her father heard about Perkins School for the Blind in Boston and contacted the Director there. In 1887, the Director sent one of his best students, Anne Sullivan, to help Helen. Anne worked hard to enable her to communicate.

In 1890, Helen's formal education started at Horace Mann School for the deaf in Boston. In 1892, she went to Wright Humason School for the deaf in New York. Helen improved her communication skills and studied regular academic subjects. She then decided to go to college, and attended Cambridge School for Young Ladies in 1896. Later, she studied at Radcliffe College. Here she learnt Braille, which was a new technique for reading. She also learnt how to type. Anne Sullivan was always with Helen. She sat with Helen in class and helped her by interpreting lectures and texts. In 1904, Helen received her BA degree with Honours from Radcliffe College.

Helen spent her life helping disabled people to learn. She died on June 1, 1968.

Their achievement was celebrated worldwide. Edmund Hillary was later knighted by Queen Elizabeth II, and Tenzing Norgay became an international hero. Their climb remains an inspiration for adventurers and mountaineers across the globe, proving that with courage and perseverance, even the greatest heights can be reached.

11. The Greedy Farmer's Lesson

In a small village, there lived a greedy farmer named Alam. He had plenty of land and cattle, but he always wanted more. One day, a wise old man told him about a magical offer. "If you run from sunrise to sunset, all the land you cover will be yours," he said. Alam, driven by greed, decided to take the challenge. The next morning, he started running as fast as he could, marking the land he covered. By midday, he was exhausted but continued running, thinking of the wealth he would gain. As the sun began to set, Alam collapsed from exhaustion, realizing he had covered far more land than he could ever manage. The wise old man appeared and said, "Greed leads to destruction. Be content with what you have." Alam learned his lesson and returned to his village, sharing his newfound wisdom with others.

12. Patriotism

Patriotism is the love and devotion for one's country. It inspires individuals to work for the betterment of their nation and uphold its values. A true patriot respects the country's history, culture, and traditions while striving to contribute to its progress. Patriotism is not just about celebrating national holidays or waving flags; it is about taking responsibility for the nation's welfare. Patriots are willing to make sacrifices for their country, whether by serving in the military, participating in community service, or simply being responsible citizens. Patriotism fosters unity and strengthens the bond among people, regardless of their differences. It encourages us to protect our country's sovereignty and work towards peace and prosperity. In times of crisis, patriots stand together to overcome challenges. True patriotism is selfless and rooted in a genuine desire to see the nation thrive. It is a virtue that every citizen should cherish and practice.
